About This Report

This reproducible report documents the data preparation, scale construction, descriptive statistics, reliability analyses, primary ANOVA analyses, and figures for this study. The report is intended to accompany a manuscript-style research paper and provides a transparent record of how the analyses were conducted using Quarto and R. Rather than serving as the final paper itself, this report demonstrates the analytical workflow used to generate the reported results from the data set.

Study Overview

This study examined how scenario type, attachment style, and cultural orientation influence relationship attributions in romantic relationships. Participants were randomly assigned to read either a positive or negative relationship scenario and then completed measures of relationship attributions, attachment style, and cultural orientation.

The primary dependent variable was Relationship Attribution Measure (RAM) scores. Two separate 2 × 2 between-subjects ANOVAs were conducted. The first examined the effects of scenario type (positive vs. negative) and attachment group (secure vs. insecure) on RAM scores. The second examined the effects of scenario type (positive vs. negative) and culture group (low vs. high cultural orientation) on RAM scores.

The primary analyses tested whether scenario type, attachment style, cultural orientation, and their interactions predicted differences in relationship attribution scores.

Create Cultural Orientation Median Split

Participants were divided into low and high culture groups using a median split of the culture scale scores. These groups were used in the culture ANOVA model.

# Compute median culture score
